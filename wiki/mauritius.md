# 🇲🇺 How to get licensed: Mauritius

[All countries](<https://github.com/jasiek/webchirp/wiki/Home>)

_2026-09-30_

## Official procedure

1. Obtain the Mauritius Amateur Radio Society (MARS) competency certificate, or an internationally recognized equivalent at Class B level. [ICTA amateur licensing guide](<https://www.icta.mu/radiocom-radio-amateur-guidelines/>)
2. Submit the prescribed RA24 Class B application to the Information and Communication Technologies Authority with proof of competence, ID or birth certificate, station details and antenna diagrams if applicable. [Class B application requirements](<https://www.icta.mu/radiocom-radio-amateur-guidelines/>)

## Licensing authority

[Information and Communication Technologies Authority (ICTA)](<https://lab.icta.mu/radiocom-radio-amateur-guidelines/>)

## Cost

Class B RA24 has no application fee and an annual licence fee of Mauritian Rs 300; Class A RA23 is Rs 500 annually. [Sources](<https://www.icta.mu/documents/2026/03/Lic_regulations.pdf>)

## Processing time

A current processing time could not be verified from the linked sources; ask the authority before applying.

No qualifying applicant report was found.

## Additional requirements

- Applicants must be at least 16; ICTA type approval is required for the equipment, and ICTA advises waiting for licence approval before buying or installing it. [Sources](<https://www.icta.mu/radiocom-radio-amateur-guidelines/>)
- The Class B licence is valid for five years and permits chiefly VHF/UHF operation. [Sources](<https://www.icta.mu/radiocom-radio-amateur-guidelines/>)

## CEPT and visiting operators

This is not listed as a current CEPT member or T/R 61-01 implementer. CEPT visitor privileges are not established here; ask the destination authority before operating.

[CEPT membership](<https://docdb.cept.org/implementation/overview>) · [T/R 61-01](<https://docdb.cept.org/document/925>) · [Implementation](<https://docdb.cept.org/implementation/925>)
